My father kept the sea. He had the eye
that finds the fish where sky and water meet;
he met the tide each day; he did defy
the wave that hit the ship, the dark, the sleet.
He is at sea this dusk. I keep awake;
I keep the tea but wait, the tide my guide,
then if my faith might fetch him back, I make
the teacup hiss, the chair set by my side.
The dark is thick. The teacup keeps the hiss.
I hear the deck; I hear the pier; I wait.
The dead ask this: that we recite the kiss,
the arm that fed me, then the arm that ate.
My father kept the deep. The deep kept him.
I keep the tea. The teacup hiss grew dim.
